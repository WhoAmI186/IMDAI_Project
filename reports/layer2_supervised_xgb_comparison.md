## 1. Objective

This controlled experiment answers a foundational architectural question:
> *"Does a supervised XGBoost attack classifier provide superior, more generalizable attack-detection performance than the current unsupervised physical-relationship XGBoost regression baseline?"*

The goal of this experiment is **objective scientific comparison**, not automatic architectural replacement.

### Architectural Context:
- **Current Layer 2 (Unsupervised Baseline):** Models normal physical relationships ($P_{\text{ac}} \approx \eta P_{\text{dc}}$, dual anemometer speeds, dual nacelle temperatures, air vs. cell temperatures) using XGBoost regression trained **strictly on normal telemetry** (`20260225_normal`). Physical anomalies are detected via prediction residuals without assuming the cause is necessarily a cyberattack.
- **Experimental Branch (Supervised Classifier):** Trains an `XGBClassifier` to directly discriminate between normal telemetry ($y=0$) and active, physically impactful attack intervals ($y=1$).

---

## 2. Existing Unsupervised Baseline

The baseline against which the supervised model is compared consists of the four finalized Layer 2 physical relationship regressors:
1. **PV Inverter Relationship:** `pv_m_inverter_dc_power` $\to$ `pv_m_inverter_ac_power` ($P_{99.5} = 10.3677\text{ kW}$)
2. **Wind Anemometer Relationship:** `wind_m_wind_speed_a` $\to$ `wind_m_wind_speed_b` ($P_{99.5} = 1.2283\text{ m/s}$)
3. **Wind Temperature Relationship:** `wind_m_temperature_a` $\to$ `wind_m_temperature_b` ($P_{99.5} = 1.9437\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$)
4. **PV Thermal Relationship:** `pv_m_temp_air` $\to$ `pv_m_cell_temperature` ($P_{99.5} = 15.2889\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$)

These models remain frozen in `models/layer2_*_xgb.json` and were evaluated strictly on the 5 adversarial campaigns (153,196 sequences, 304 qualifying impactful episodes).

## 3. Supervised Methodology

Two distinct supervised classification models were designed and evaluated:

1. **Primary Supervised Classifier (`raw_14`):**
   - **Inputs:** The authoritative 14 raw physical measurements from Config A:
     `pv_m_temp_air`, `pv_m_poa_direct`, `pv_m_wind_speed`, `pv_m_poa_diffuse`, `pv_m_cell_temperature`, `pv_m_inverter_ac_power`, `pv_m_inverter_dc_power`, `pv_c_on_off`, `wind_m_power`, `wind_m_pressure`, `wind_m_wind_speed_a`, `wind_m_wind_speed_b`, `wind_m_temperature_a`, `wind_m_temperature_b`.
   - **Target:** $y \in \{0, 1\}$, predicting continuous attack probability $P(\text{attack})$ via logistic loss.
   - **Model Checkpoint:** `models/layer2_supervised_xgb.json`.

2. **Secondary Supervised Classifier (`residuals_4`):**
   - **Inputs:** The four continuous physical residuals generated by the frozen Layer 2 regression models:
     `pv_inverter_residual`, `wind_speed_residual`, `wind_temperature_residual`, `pv_thermal_residual`.
   - **Purpose:** Diagnostic ablation testing whether non-linear combinations of physical residuals can distinguish cyberattacks from normal operating noise.
   - **Model Checkpoint:** `models/layer2_supervised_residuals_xgb.json`.

## 4. Label Construction

Labels are constructed using the verified ground-truth pipeline without lookahead:
- **Normal Telemetry (`20260225_normal`):** Ground-truth label $y = 0$ for all sequences.
- **Adversarial Telemetry (5 Multi-Agent Campaigns):** Labeled using **Strategy D** ($t_{59}$ causal sequence endpoint):
  $$y_i = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } t_{59} \in [t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{stop}}] \text{ of a qualifying impactful Solar/Wind attack} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
- **Qualifying Criteria:** Target subsystem table $\in \{\text{'pv\_process\_data'}, \text{'wind\_process\_data'}\}$ AND verified physical impact observed ($\text{delta\_observed\_during\_active} == \text{True}$).
- Battery attacks and unverified/zero-impact perturbations remain labeled as $0$ in the primary scope benchmark.

## 5. Campaign-Aware Split (No Random Window Leakage)

> [!CAUTION]
> **Zero Random Splitting Protocol:**
> Randomly splitting sliding windows would cause catastrophic temporal data leakage: adjacent windows from the same attack episode differ by only 1 time step (~0.53s) and share 59 overlapping observations. A random split would allow the tree classifier to memorize attack-specific temporal signatures across train and test partitions.

Instead, a **strictly grouped, campaign-aware split** was enforced:

| Partition | Campaigns & Datasets Included | Total Sequences | Positive Sequences ($y=1$) | Prevalence | Qualifying Episodes |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **TRAIN** | `20260228_multi_openai` (OpenAI GPT-5.3)<br>`20260301_multi_sonnet` (Claude Sonnet 1)<br>`20260302_multi_minimax` (MiniMax M2.5)<br>+ `20260225_normal` (Train 80%) | **105,771** | **4,988** | **4.72%** | **230 episodes** |
| **VALIDATION** | `20260301_multi_google` (Google Gemini 3.1 Pro)<br>+ `20260225_normal` (Val 20%) | **44,308** | **212** | **0.48%** | **10 episodes** |
| **HELD-OUT TEST** | `20260303_multi_sonnet` (Claude Sonnet 2) | **14,990** | **1,167** | **7.79%** | **64 episodes** |

Zero recording runs or attack sessions are shared across partitions.

## 6. Class Distribution & Imbalance Handling

The cyber-physical telemetry stream is heavily imbalanced:
- **Training Partition ($N=105,771$):**
  - Negatives ($y=0$): 100,783 sequences (95.28%)
  - Positives ($y=1$): 4,988 sequences (4.72%)
  - Ratio: $100,783 / 4,988 = 20.2051$
- **Class Weighting (`scale_pos_weight`):**
  Set strictly to **$20.2051$** based on the training partition counts:
  $$\text{scale\_pos\_weight} = \frac{N_{\text{train\_neg}}}{N_{\text{train\_pos}}}$$
  Test labels were **never** accessed to compute class weights.

## 16. Final Answers & Architecture Recommendation

### Explicit Answers to Experimental Questions:

#### 1. Does supervised XGBoost improve attack detection?
**Marginally on in-distribution campaigns, but NOT reliably overall.**
On the single held-out test campaign (`20260303_multi_sonnet`), the supervised classifier achieved a higher F1 score (0.1411 vs. 0.0693) and higher episode detection (29.69% vs. 12.50%). However, this comes at the cost of a **2.6x higher False Positive Rate (19.10% vs. 7.28%)**.

#### 2. Does it generalize to unseen attack campaigns?
**NO. It suffers from severe, brittle overfitting to known attack patterns.**
In Leave-One-Campaign-Out cross-validation, the supervised classifier suffered a catastrophic failure on the Google Gemini campaign (`20260301_multi_google`), collapsing to **$\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.4448$** (worse than random guessing) and **$\text{PR-AUC} = 0.0043$**. On other campaigns, maintaining detection required an impossible **FPR of 65% to 87%**.

#### 3. What does it gain compared with physical-residual modeling?
It gains the ability to learn empirical non-linear correlations across all 14 channels simultaneously, allowing it to capture multi-sensor setpoint combinations that occur during the specific attacks seen during training.

#### 4. What does it lose?
- **Universal Physical Invariance:** It cannot detect novel attack vectors that manipulate sensors in ways not present in the training set.
- **Explainability:** It outputs an abstract probability $P(\text{attack})$ rather than a physical residual with units (e.g. "$12.4\text{ kW}$ DC/AC inverter divergence").
- **False Positive Control:** Because it lacks physical conservation constraints, operational shifts in weather or demand trigger massive false positive spikes (up to 87% FPR).

#### 5. Should it remain experimental or proceed toward the architecture?
### **FINAL CLASSIFICATION: EXPERIMENTAL ONLY**

The supervised XGBoost classification approach is **strictly classified as EXPERIMENTAL ONLY**.

It **MUST NOT replace** the current unsupervised physical-relationship regression pipeline. The unsupervised regression baseline remains the authoritative, scientifically sound implementation for Layer 2 because it models physical laws independently of adversarial assumptions and guarantees stable, bounded operational false alarm rates.

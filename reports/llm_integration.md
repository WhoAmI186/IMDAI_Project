## 1. Executive Summary

This report documents the architectural design, implementation, and rigorous evaluation of the **LLM Investigation Layer** (`src/llm/`). 

The LLM is positioned strictly downstream of the deterministic **Power System Digital Twin**. Its purpose is to:
- Translate structured Digital Twin engineering states and evidence into actionable operator summaries.
- Propose plausible physical and cyber-physical explanations.
- Guide control room and forensic investigation.
- Provide clean, typed JSON output formatted for future dashboard integration.

### Core Architectural Guarantees:
1. **Downstream Isolation:** The LLM receives **only** sanitized, compact outputs from the Digital Twin (`LLMInvestigationInput`). It does not consume raw 129-column PMU streams, and ground-truth dataset markers (`Attack`, `Natural`, `NoEvents`) are strictly excluded from runtime prompts.
2. **OBSERVATIONS != HYPOTHESES:** The LLM strictly separates verified factual observations generated by the Digital Twin from speculative hypotheses formulated by the model.
3. **Zero Cyberattack Classification Authority:** The LLM does **not** possess the authority to classify an event as a confirmed cyberattack. Any cyber manipulation is reported strictly as a hypothesis with supporting rationale and confidence ratings.
4. **Frozen Baselines:** Layer 1 TCN-AE, Layer 2 XGBoost models, Evidence Fusion, checkpoints, thresholds, and Digital Twin logic remain 100% frozen with verified SHA-256 hash preservation.

## 4. Prompt Engineering & Grounding Principles

The system prompt in [`src/llm/prompt_builder.py`](file:///c:/Users/LENOVO/Downloads/IMDAI%20project/src/llm/prompt_builder.py) sets rigorous boundaries:

```
You are a Smart Grid event investigation assistant.

You receive structured evidence generated by a deterministic Digital Twin and ML-based anomaly detection pipeline.

Your job is to explain the observed event using only the supplied evidence.

Do not invent measurements, topology states, breaker states, or events.

Do not claim that an event is definitely a cyberattack.

When cyber-related explanations are possible, describe them as hypotheses rather than facts.

Separate observed evidence from interpretation.

If the evidence is insufficient, explicitly state that the cause cannot be determined.
```

### Prompt Guardrails:
1. **Zero Hallucination:** The model is prohibited from inventing voltage values, frequency deviations, relay trip flags, or attacker identities not present in the input context.
2. **Observation vs Hypothesis Separation:** Factual measurements and Digital Twin findings are restricted to `observed_evidence`. Inferences regarding potential cyber tampering or protective relay actions are quarantined in `possible_explanations`.
3. **No Defensive Certainty:** Speculation cannot be framed as certainty (e.g. *"This is an attack by APT29"* is forbidden; *"Unauthorized remote trip command to circuit breaker is a plausible hypothesis"* is required).

## 9. Known Limitations

1. **In-Band FDI Without Topology Inconsistency:** When an attack manipulates analog currents within normal operating ranges without tripping breakers or breaking Kirchhoff's laws, the LLM correctly categorizes the event as a statistical anomaly (`STATISTICAL_DISTURBANCE_NORMAL_TOPOLOGY`), but cannot confirm cyber malice without network or host telemetry.
2. **Local Hardware Constraints for 8B Weights:** Running Qwen3-8B unquantized in 16-bit requires ~16 GB VRAM, exceeding the 6 GB capacity of laptop GPUs like the RTX 4050. Running locally on this hardware requires 4-bit quantization (e.g. AWQ/GGUF or BitsAndBytes) or offloading to a local inference server (Ollama / vLLM).
3. **No External Knowledge Retrieval (RAG Not Yet Implemented):** The LLM currently relies solely on the provided prompt context and cannot yet query external substation schematics, relay coordination curves, or historical incident tickets.
